# Signal Lanes: Where to Look and How to Date It

Use this when building the evidence ledger. Prefer primary sources (the company's own posts, filings, archived pages) over secondary retellings.

## Dating any signal

- **Wayback Machine** (`web.archive.org/web/*/domain.com*`): when a page, price, product, or claim first appeared. The single most useful dating tool.
- **Post timestamps** on the company's own social accounts, blog, and changelog.
- **Press dates**: use the article date, and note whether it reports a past event.
- **Filings and registries**: company registrations, trademark filings (USPTO, EUIPO), SEC filings for public companies or crowdfunding raises.

If a signal can only be dated to a year, say so. Don't invent a month.

## Lanes

### Product & offer
- Archived homepage and pricing pages by quarter: hero product, price, bundles, guarantees.
- Product launch posts, changelogs, release notes.
- Review counts over time on the store, Amazon, or app stores (review velocity is a usable sales proxy).

### Content & social
- The account's own top posts by engagement; note format (series, UGC, founder-led, tutorial).
- The first posts that broke their usual engagement range: these often sit right before an inflection.
- Creator and collaborator posts that tagged the brand around spikes.

### Paid
- **Meta Ad Library** and **TikTok Creative Center**: active ad count, longest-running ads (long-running usually means profitable), repeated hooks.
- **Google Ads Transparency Center**: whether and where they buy search.
- Treat ad volume as a signal of confidence in a channel, not proof of results.

### Search & AI answers
- **Google Trends** for the brand name: the cleanest free proxy for "people search for you by name" (stage 50).
- Pages that rank for category terms; when they were published (Wayback).
- Ask two or three AI assistants the category question and note which sources they cite.

### Community
- Owned groups (Discord, Facebook, Slack, subreddits), size over time if visible.
- Events, pop-ups, ambassador or rep programmes, referral offers and when they launched.
- Customer co-creation: products named or shaped by community input.

### Distribution & partners
- Retail and marketplace launches (press releases, retailer pages).
- Integrations, affiliate programmes, collaborations and limited drops.

### Press & capital
- Funding rounds (Crunchbase, press releases): date, amount, lead.
- Major press moments. Check whether growth bent before or after them; press often follows growth.

## Picking the growth proxy

Best to worst, use the first one you can source:

1. Reported revenue or customer counts (filings, founder interviews with dates).
2. Web traffic estimates (Similarweb and similar; label as estimates).
3. Branded search trend (Google Trends).
4. Review or rating count velocity.
5. Follower counts over time (archived profile pages).
6. Headcount over time (archived team pages, job posts).

State the proxy you used and its weakness in one line.
