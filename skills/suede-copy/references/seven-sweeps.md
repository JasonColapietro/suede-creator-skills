# Seven Sweeps Edit Method

Use this when the job is editing copy that already exists rather than writing it
fresh. Adapted from the copy-editing skill in
[marketingskills](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills) by Corey
Haines (MIT; see `licenses/marketingskills-MIT.txt`).

Rules for every sweep: keep the core message, give each edit a reason, and keep
the author's voice. One dimension per pass catches what a single mixed pass
misses. After each sweep, re-check the earlier sweeps so a later edit has not
broken them.

## The Sweeps

1. **Clarity.** Can the reader follow it? Mark confusing structure, unclear
   pronouns, jargon, ambiguity, and missing context before rewriting. Keep one
   main idea per section and keep the copy addressed to the reader.
2. **Voice and tone.** Read it aloud. Mark shifts between formal and casual,
   mixed "we" and "the company", stray technical language, and humor that
   appears in one place only. Smooth the transitions.
3. **So what.** For every statement, ask "so what?" Features need the benefit
   bridge ("which means..."). Company achievements must connect to something the
   reader gets.
4. **Prove it.** List every claim that needs proof and check whether proof sits
   nearby: named testimonials, case studies, data, third-party validation,
   guarantees, logos, review scores. Flag "trusted by thousands" and
   "industry-leading" style lines. Claims the operator supplied about their own
   business stay as written and go to `Claims to verify`; only invented or
   provably false claims get cut.
5. **Specificity.** Replace vague verbs (improve, enhance, optimize), generic
   lines, and round numbers that feel made up with real numbers, timeframes, and
   examples from the source. Cut what cannot be made specific; it is usually
   filler. Never invent the number.
6. **Heightened emotion.** Does it make the reader feel the before state, the
   frustration with alternatives, or the relief of the fix? Add texture with
   micro-stories and concrete detail, never with manipulation or false urgency.
7. **Zero risk.** Near each CTA, list every reason to hesitate and check the
   copy answers it: guarantees, trials, "cancel anytime", proof next to the
   button, a clear next step, privacy notes. Replace vague "Contact us" with the
   actual next step.

After sweep 7, run back through 6 to 1 once more.

## Expert Panel Score (high-stakes copy)

Use after the sweeps for launch copy, pricing pages, high-traffic landing pages,
and sales pages; optional for email and ads; skip for minor edits.

1. Seat 3 to 5 personas suited to the surface. Landing page: conversion
   copywriter, UX writer, target customer, brand strategist. Email sequence:
   email specialist, copywriter, deliverability reviewer, target customer.
   Long-form sales page: direct-response copywriter, skeptical buyer, editor,
   SEO reviewer.
2. Each persona scores 1 to 10 in its area and names the specific fix.
3. Revise the lowest scores first, then re-score.
4. Stop when every persona is at 7 or above and the panel average is 8 or above.

| Score | Meaning |
| ----- | ------- |
| 9-10 | Publish-ready |
| 7-8 | Strong, minor tweaks |
| 5-6 | Clear gaps, another pass |
| 3-4 | Major revision |
| 1-2 | Rethink the approach |

The panel is a diagnostic. It does not replace the 70-point Ship Gate in
`SKILL.md`, and the personas are simulated lenses, not real reviewers.
